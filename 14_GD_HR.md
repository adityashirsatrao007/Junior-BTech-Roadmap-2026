# 14 — Group Discussion + HR + Communication (research-backed)

Mass recruiters (TCS **NQT**, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture) run a **three-round shape**:
online test (aptitude + English + coding/pseudocode) → technical interview → **GD/HR**.
The last round kills more offers than any coding question — because it's the easiest
to prepare AND the easiest to lose. This file is built from 2026 placement-prep
sources, not vibes (sources at the bottom).

**When you start it:** the *foundation* is today — communication is a daily-vitamin
skill. The *sprint* is the 4 weeks before any drive.

---

## 1. What the HR/GD round actually tests

Recruiters score four things, in order:
1. **Communication** — can they understand you in a noisy room / across a video call?
2. **Flexibility & stability** — willing to relocate? any shift/technology? bond? *(hesitation here is the most common silent rejection)*
3. **Honesty & judgment** — you know your resume, you don't bluff skills.
4. **Personality & enthusiasm** — warm, concise, and genuinely interested in the company.

Company-specific quirks (from 2026 prep guides):
- **TCS** — clubbed technical + managerial + HR panel; adaptability + project clarity win.
- **Infosys** — *communication and learnability* are decisive. Speak clearly.
- **Wipro** — has a **written essay** round (200–300 words) people forget about; don't.

---

## 2. The 6 classic HR questions (have cold answers)

1. **Tell me about yourself** — 60 seconds: name branch → 1 tech skill → 1 project with a result → why this company. No family history, no poems.
2. **Why this company?** — reference their specifics: TCS ILP training / Infosys Mysuru campus / Wipro onboarding, global exposure, learning culture. Tie to "strong early foundation".
3. **Why should we hire you?** — your golden 3: skills + one project outcome + flexibility.
4. **Relocate / any shift / any technology / bond?** — clear YES + one-line reasoning. Never hesitate.
5. **Where do you see yourself in 5 years?** — "growing into a senior engineer/team lead at [company], having delivered on real client projects." Not "CEO".
6. **Tell me about a time you worked in a team / a failure / a conflict** — use **STAR** (below).

---

## 3. STAR method — your behavioural answer template

| Letter | Means | Your 60–90 second answer |
|--------|-------|--------------------------|
| **S** | Situation | set the scene in one line |
| **T** | Task | what were you responsible for |
| **A** | Action | what YOU specifically did (I, not we) |
| **R** | Result | measured outcome: %, count, time saved |

Prepare **5 STAR stories** you can reuse: (1) a team project, (2) leading something,
(3) a failure + what you learned, (4) conflict resolution, (5) going beyond the
requirement. Write each as 4 bullets in your daily log. Practise them OUT LOUD —
silent reading doesn't build fluency.

---

## 4. GD (if a drive includes it)

One topic, 6–10 people, 15 minutes. Scorecard: **participation build > dominating**.

- **Never sit silent.** The silence itself is a failure signal.
- Structure your views as a **3-point answer** in the first minute.
- **Listen + build + differ politely** ("I'd add that…", "I see it differently because…").
- Give a **conclusion which unites the room** — GDs reward the summariser.
- Practice topics: "AI replacing jobs", "remote work vs office", "impact of social
  media", "IT vs core engineering", "skills vs degrees". Speak for 60s each, timed
  with a phone, alone or with friends.

---

## 5. The Wipro-style essay (don't let this surprise you)

2026 guide: practise writing a **clear, structured 200–300-word essay in 20 minutes**
on topics like technology's impact, remote work, your role model, online education.
Structure: intro (thesis) → 3 short paragraphs (argument/example/counter) → conclusion.
Think of it as your STAR for writing — same discipline.

## 6. Your 4-week pre-drive sprint (when a drive is announced)

| Week | Focus |
|------|-------|
| 1 | Daily aptitude (already running) + revise one language's fundamentals + 1 full mock |
| 2 | CS fundamentals (13_CORE_CS_THEORY) + 2–3 coding probs/day + resume ATS check |
| 3 | Project deep-dive, HR answers, 5 STAR stories, essay practice; two full mocks |
| 4 | DAILY out-loud mock interviews (technical + HR); fix weak spots; rest the day before |

---

## 7. Interview-day logistics (small things that derail people)

- Multiple resume copies + photo ID + marksheets/certificates.
- Formal dress. Reach/join 30 min early; test video+mic the night before for online rounds.
- Pen, notepad, water; phone silent.
- Between rounds, review your **one-page revision sheet**, don't panic.

> **Make the one-page sheet NOW** (put a copy in `resume/`): OOP 4 pillars + examples,
> top-10 SQL queries, normalization forms, process/thread/deadlock, TCP vs UDP + OSI,
> your project in 2 lines, your 5 STAR stories one line each.
